## Lumen 1.6: record a screen, a window or a game straight into the edit

### Record

The red dot in the title bar, **Ctrl Shift R**, or the command palette. [How it works, and its limits →](https://github.com/Mas-inx/lumen-ai-video-editor/blob/main/docs/RECORDING.md)

- **Pick the source from live pictures:** any screen, any open window, or a region you drag over either.
- **Up to 4K at 60 frames a second**, at the source's own size or scaled up or down, in H.264, HEVC or AV1.
- **Expert settings:** graphics card or processor, quality or an exact bitrate, key-frame interval, pointer in or out of the picture, countdown.
- **Sound, your choice:** everything the computer plays, only the recorded window's app, or none. The microphone and the camera are recorded as clips of their own.
- **On the timeline when it stops:** the recording on the main track, the camera in the corner above it, the microphone on a voice track, lined up and grouped.
- **Pause, mark a moment, stop** from the record window, the title bar, or keys that work while another app is in front (Ctrl Alt R, P and M).
- **Lumen stays out of a screen recording.**

### Built not to lose a take

- **Any graphics card.** The encoder is tried with real frames at the recording's size first: the codec you asked for on the graphics card, then another codec on the card, then the processor.
- **An encoder that stops part-way**, or a window that changes size, ends the file there and the recording carries on in a new one within about half a second.
- **A recording survives a crash.** If Lumen closes unexpectedly while recording, the next start offers to recover what was recorded.

### Clicks as data

A recording keeps where the pointer was and when it clicked.

- **Zoom on clicks** (Inspector › Clicks) pushes in on each click, pans between clicks close together and eases back out.
- **Highlight clicks** draws a pulse where each click landed.
- Both are ordinary keyframes and clips afterwards, and one undo takes them off.

### The Copilot can record

“Record my browser while I show the bug, then zoom on the clicks and add captions.”

- **Nine tools** — `list_capture_sources`, `start_recording`, `recording_status`, `pause_recording`, `add_recording_marker`, `stop_recording`, `zoom_on_clicks`, `highlight_clicks`, `record_studio_take` — and a *screen-recording* skill. **133 tools** in all now.
- **It asks first.** The first time, Lumen asks you to allow screen recording for the session. A red dot in the title bar shows whenever a recording runs.
- **Agents on Lumen's MCP server** (Claude Code, Codex, your own) have the same tools, and the same question is asked.

### Film a GS Cinematic Studio take live

`record_studio_take` records the game's window while the studio plays the take with every panel hidden, then puts the clip on the timeline trimmed to exactly the take, with the game's sound and the studio's cue sheet. It needs a studio that has the `film_take` tool, on the same computer, with the game windowed or borderless. Frame ingest stays the frame-exact route.

### What was and wasn't tested

This was built and tested on one laptop with Intel graphics. There, a recording at the source's own size kept every frame the source sent at 60 frames a second; scaling a smaller source up to 4K while recording held about 50, because the picture is resized on the processor (record at native size and export at 4K instead).

Not tested on real hardware or in real use for this release: NVIDIA and AMD cards (they go through the same test-and-fall-back), a true 3840×2160 source, a real game, `film_take` inside the game, Windows 10, and more than one monitor. If a recording misbehaves, *Lumen menu › Graphics and encoding › Copy report* and what the record window said are the things to send.
